Tata Technologies Decoded

Sheet 1

The gist in one minute

Tata Technologies (TTL) is an engineering services company. Car makers, aircraft suppliers and machinery makers hire it to design, build and test their products. For about 30 years it was paid mostly for engineers' time. The Investor Day was about changing that: take on whole products for a fixed price, use AI to deliver them cheaper than the price, and keep the difference.

Think of it as a shift from subcontractor to general contractor. A subcontractor rents out carpenters by the hour. A general contractor delivers the finished house for an agreed price, and carries the risk. TTL wants to be the second one for vehicles, aircraft and heavy machines.

1. The auto industry is resetting

Western carmakers face Chinese rivals that build a new model in 18 to 24 months, against 36 to 60 months for them. They are cutting jobs and want fewer, bigger outsourcing partners who can run an entire vehicle programme.

2. Land, expand, replicate

Win a Full Vehicle Programme (FVP). Use it to sell more: plant set-up, software, lifecycle services. Then take the same playbook to aerospace and industrial heavy machinery.

3. AI as the margin lever

Because TTL is paid for outcomes and not hours, time saved by AI becomes profit instead of a discount. Management claims 30 years of programme knowledge is what makes its AI better than a generic tool.

The playbook: land, expand, replicate

This is the same idea in the keynote, the software deck and the aerospace deck. Pick a step to see the evidence they offered.

Why they think the market is bigger than it looks

Traditional outsourced engineering is a $100Bn market, of which TTL can serve about $15Bn. By counting the whole product lifecycle (plant, aftermarket, services) the deck redraws it as $230Bn and $33–34Bn.

TTL's FY26 revenue of $0.62Bn is about 1.9% of the $33–34Bn serviceable market. The FY30 target of $1.1–1.3Bn would be about 3.5%, assuming that market stays near today's size (my arithmetic). Source: Product Realisation deck, citing BCG, NASSCOM–Everest, Grand View Research.

Sheet 5

What this means for Indian IT

TTL sells to a product company's engineering budget, not its IT budget. That makes it a cousin of TCS and Infosys, not a clone. But the forces in the decks are the same ones reshaping the whole sector.

Hours are becoming outcomes

Clients now ask vendors for the same work at 25–30% less (Persistent's description). TCS says about 80% of its finance, HR and business-services contracts are outcome-based. TTL says all 8 of its in-flight programmes are outcome-led.

Domain context is the moat

Anyone can rent a frontier model. TTL argues its edge is the tacit knowledge from 35+ full-vehicle programmes. The same logic favours specialists in any vertical over generalist coders.

Fewer, bigger partners

The deck sorts suppliers into 100+ "capacity" partners, 10–15 "competency" partners and a few "full capability" partners. Mandates concentrate at the top. That is bad news for the middle of the pack.

Try it: who keeps the AI savings?

A piece of work takes 100 hours, billed at 100, with cost of 84 (a 16% margin, close to TTL's today). AI cuts the hours needed. What happens to profit depends on how you are paid.

Teaching model, not company data. Algebra: outcome pricing beats hourly billing exactly when the client's price cut is smaller than your productivity gain. If you cannot predict the gain, you are exposed on the downside.

The test is whether it can hold prices while its customers are cutting costs.

What a bull would point to

  • FVP wins up about 5× in a quarter ($40Mn to $195Mn), pipeline up about 9×

  • Margin path to ~20% with a history of ~18%
  • Payout above 50% and ROE rebuilding toward 20%

What a sceptic would point to

  • USD revenue was flat for three years ($618Mn, $611Mn, $620Mn), then a jump is being promised
  • FY26 profit fell to ₹547 Cr from ₹677 Cr; Q2 FY27 profit rose only 3.5% on 28% revenue growth

  • TCV is not revenue. The $138Mn deal runs 24 months, roughly $69Mn a year, about 11% of FY26 revenue
  • Over 95% of revenue is from top global OEMs, the same group cutting jobs and posting losses
  • The FY30 bridge includes M&A; organic growth may be lower than 15–20%

Sheet 6B · Addendum

What went wrong so far

The Investor Day was a recovery story. This is what it is recovering from. The stock listed at ₹1,200 in November 2023 (IPO price ₹500) and sat at ₹728 on 20 Nov 2026, about 39% below the listing price and about 46% above the IPO price (my arithmetic).

Profit after tax, ₹ Cr

FY26 profit fell 19.3% on higher employee costs, acquisition integration and temporary programme headwinds, per reports of the results. Source: Screener.in.

Five things that slipped

  • Demand. USD revenue was $618Mn, $611Mn and $620Mn across FY24 to FY26. Large-deal ramp-ups were delayed and decision cycles were long.
  • Margin. EBITDA margin dipped to 14.1% in Q3 FY26 and was 15.5% for the year, against about 18% in the past.
  • Returns. ROE fell from 21.1% (FY24) to 13.9% (FY26).
  • People. Attrition rose from 13.8% to 15.1% in one quarter of FY26.
  • Customers. JLR IT restoration risk, VinFast rolling off the $50Mn+ tier, and the top three accounts still Tata Motors group names.

Sheet 7

Before you quote the decks

Things I noticed while cross-reading. None is damning, but each is worth clearing up before it goes into a post or a model.

  • 4× or 5×? The keynote says large-deal wins grew "4×" on one slide and "5×" on another. $390Mn+ against $84Mn is 4.6×.
  • "Double in three years" needs 26%. The software deck says 20–25% CAGR and "double". From $100Mn in FY27E, 20% gives 1.73× and 25% gives 1.95× by FY30.
  • Aerospace growth, three windows. "~40% five-year CAGR", "~8× from FY22 to FY26" (about 68% a year) and "12.5% compound quarterly growth" since Q3 FY24 use different periods. Ask for revenue in dollars.
  • Which period are the five big wins? The keynote labels them "H1'27"; the CFO deck says "last four quarters".
  • The non-auto target is a low bar. Aerospace + heavy machinery is already 19.6% of services revenue. The FY30 goal is ">20%", though the long-term ambition is about 30%.
  • Flagship software share. The $20Mn software part of the $138Mn deal is 14.5%, below the 20–40% the software deck calls typical for an FVP. The US vehicle deal ($6Mn of $17Mn, 35%) is inside the range.
  • Redacted charts. The CFO deck states that some chart values are redacted and bars are representative. Do not read exact figures off them.
  • Customer concentration is not given. The target is "<20% from any one customer", but the decks never state today's number. Ask.
  • ES-Tec. Brokers disagree on Q2 sequential growth (JPMorgan 4.3% constant-currency, 7% excluding ES-Tec; Goldman 1.9%), and ES-Tec's performance remains a worry. Ask how much of the +22% is acquired.
