THE SHORT VERSION

The index rested. The buyers did not.

The Nifty fell 0.7% for the week, its first weekly loss since the October washout, and Friday’s 1.1% drop came from banks and metals. Underneath, the flows held: FIIs bought on every session, and domestic institutions were net buyers for the week for the first time in November. Breadth is still the missing piece.

1
WHERE IS THE MONEY FLOWING?

Still in through the front door. FIIs bought ₹17,630 cr in four sessions, net buyers every day, taking November to ₹1,29,579 cr. DIIs net bought ₹6,333 cr after three weeks of selling.

2
WHICH INDEX IS DOING WELL?

Banks, and now pharma. Nifty Private Bank tops StockScans index strength at 93.3. Nifty Pharma scores 90.0 and sits 0.4% from its one-year high. Nifty IT climbed to 76.8 after a 4.6% week.

3
HOW IS KOSPI DOING?

It fell 6.2% on Wednesday to 4,697.51, its lowest close since mid-January, then jumped 4.3% on Friday to 4,915.68 on foreign buying. Up 0.9% on the week, still 46.1% below its June peak.

4
WHAT IS HAPPENING TO SOXX?

Whipsawed. Up about 14% on Monday on Nvidia leadership news, down 8.3% on Tuesday, $127.98 at Wednesday’s close. Still about 32% below end-September.

5
SIGNS OF REGIME CHANGE?

In leadership, yes. In participation, still no. 45.4% of stocks sit above their 200-day EMA, and 148 of 247 industries are underperforming, up from 127 a week ago.

6
WHERE ARE WE IN THE RELAY?

Still leg one, with a first stir from the feeder. The one-month return still falls down the cap ladder, from +16.8% for Nifty Bank to +2.5% for the Microcap 250 (Exhibit 1), but DIIs bought this week.

FLOWS AND INDICES

Where the money is flowing

EXHIBIT 2 FOUR WEEKS OF FII BUYING; THE FIRST WEEK OF DII BUYING
NSE provisional cash-market data, daily figures summed from press reports (24 Nov was a market holiday). November to 27th: FII +₹1,29,579 cr, DII −₹55,454 cr.

The size. November stands at ₹1,29,579 crore with one session left, almost twice the previous record of ₹66,135 crore (December 2023). The pace has slowed: ₹17,630 crore this week against ₹45,960 crore in the first.

The context. Foreigners sold about ₹6.32 lakh crore between July 2025 and October 2026, so about a fifth has come back. On our arithmetic FIIs are still net sellers of roughly ₹1.5–1.6 lakh crore for 2026.

The door they used. When money arrives this fast it goes where the liquidity is, and in India that means banks. The door is still cheap: Bank Nifty trades at 14.0x earnings and 1.91x book, in the bottom tenth of its ten-year range.

The other side. Domestic institutions sold ₹61,787 crore in the first three weeks, then net bought ₹6,333 crore this week, buying on Wednesday and Thursday. One week is not a trend, but it is the signpost section 06 watches.

The good news. Friday was the broadest up day in weeks: 112 stocks rose 4% or more and only 20 fell 4%, even as the Nifty lost 1.1%. Stocks near 52-week lows fell from 168 on Wednesday to 130.

The catch. Over the week every breadth measure still slipped: above the 20-day EMA from 47.5% to 45.8%, above the 200-day from 46.5% to 45.4%, near 52-week highs from 137 to 132. Underperforming industries rose to 148 of 247. The broad market had one good day, not a good week.

April had the breadth without the foreign buyer. November has the foreign buyer without the breadth.

April’s thrust took 95.3% of stocks above the 20-day EMA, yet six months later the Nifty was lower. Breadth alone did not make a new market. The open question is whether flow alone can. Friday was a small hint: the index fell and the crowd rose.

KOSPI

KOSPI fell 6.16% on Wednesday to 4,697.51 as foreigners sold 1.95 trillion won. Friday reversed it: +4.34% to 4,915.68 and KOSDAQ +8.1%, with foreigners net buyers of 1.13 trillion won while retail investors sold 1.77 trillion. Korean brokerages had flagged 4,800 as the line to hold; the index closed the week back above it.

SOXX

SOXX rallied about 14% on Monday, fell 8.27% on Tuesday to $128.62 as Micron dropped about 11%, and closed Wednesday at $127.98. US markets were shut on Thursday and traded a half session on Friday, whose close we could not confirm. The ETF is still about 32% below its end-September level.

Why this matters for India. This was the first week of the ‘choosing India or fleeing AI’ test. Chips bounced and Korea bounced, and FIIs still bought India on every session. But Friday, the day Korea saw 1.13 trillion won of foreign buying, was also the smallest FII day of the week in India (₹1,634 cr). Watch whether that gap widens.

Leadership has changed regime. The market has not yet. Four signals in place, four mixed, two missing.
THE CASE THAT IT IS REAL

Bull markets in India have usually started like this: a foreign bid in large-cap financials at below-average valuations, a washout low behind it, and almost nobody else invited yet. Narrow breadth at the start is normal. Breadth confirms last, not first. This week domestic funds turned buyers too, and private banks score 93 while metals score 6.

THE CASE THAT IT IS A CATCH-UP TRADE

Small caps did not have a bear market this year. Microcaps are up 10.6% in 2026 and the Nifty is down 2.0%. So November may simply be the laggard closing the gap: FII-owned large caps mean-reverting after a long stretch of selling. Underperforming industries rising to 148 fits that reading.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT
  • Stocks above the 200-day EMA back over 60%. Today it is 45.4%.
  • More industries outperforming than underperforming on StockScans. Today it is 42 against 148.
  • FII buying that survives a week when chips bounce. This week was a partial pass: buying held, but slowed to ₹1,634 cr on Friday.
  • DIIs keep buying (they did this week) and the Midcap 150 clears its three-year high, now 3.8% away.

THE RELAY

Large to mid to small: how the baton travels

Think of a relay race where the runners are sorted by size. The first runner is always the large cap, because the money that starts a bull market is too big to fit anywhere else. Each handoff happens only once the next runner believes the race is real.

LEG 1 · WE ARE HERE
Large caps

FIIs buy index heavyweights, mostly financials. Breadth is thin. Domestic funds sell. Mid and small caps lag.

LEG 2
The catch-up

Next 50 and large midcaps follow: second-line banks, autos, consumer. Long-term breadth pushes past 60%.

LEG 3
Broadening

Domestic flows chase returns into mid and small-cap funds. New highs run into the hundreds.

LEG 4
Froth

Microcaps, SME listings and weak balance sheets lead. Quality lags. Nobody notices the baton has dropped.

THREE PAST HANDOFFS

2013–14. FIIs returned in September 2013 and the Sensex was at a record by early November. Midcaps joined only in 2014, when small caps returned 55% against the Nifty’s 31%.

2020–21. A then-record FPI month in November 2020 took the Nifty to new highs. Small caps did the heavy lifting in 2021: +59% against +24%.

2023. FPIs turned buyers in March–May, the Nifty made a new high by end-June, and small caps finished the year +56% against +20%.

The lag from large-cap breakout to small-cap takeover has been roughly one to two quarters. Sequencing is from our own market history, not a data pull.

WHY THIS HANDOFF MAY BE SLOWER

The next runner is not rested. In 2013, 2020 and 2023 small caps had been left for dead before the baton arrived. This time they led all year. The Smallcap 250 trades at 32.0x earnings, above its own median, while the Nifty at 21.1x is well below its. That is a 52% premium.

The feeder is stirring. Mid and small caps run on domestic flows. DIIs were net buyers of ₹6,333 crore this week after three weeks of selling, but they are still ₹55,454 crore out for November, and a heavy IPO calendar competes for the same rupees.

Sector beats size. Lenders and pharma are strong at every size: Nifty Pharma and MidSmall Healthcare both score 90.0. Capital goods, power and metals are weak at every size. The baton may pass through sectors before it passes down the cap curve.
