What the prospectus says

Glass half full, glass half empty

The same S-1 numbers, read by a bull and by a bear. Bulls and bears will both find support here, which is why the story you pick above matters more than the filing.

Growth→ input 01
Half fullFirst-half 2026 revenue was about 14× the first half of 2025. Around 1,900 enterprises now spend over $1M a year on Claude, up from under 100 a year earlier.
Half emptyGrowth slowed in Q3 as OpenAI won share, compute ran short and some clients moved to cheaper open-source models. The top five customers are 25% of revenue.
Profitability→ input 02
Half fullThe company says it made an operating profit in the latest quarter. Compute cost fell from over 250% of quarterly revenue to 48%, and every expense line is shrinking as a share of revenue.
Half emptyThe first-half operating loss was over $8B once you strip out a $72B non-cash charge (the reported loss was $80B). The cost of committed compute is still to hit future income statements.
Reinvestment→ input 03
Half full$600B+ of compute commitments lock in the capacity to grow and close the gap with OpenAI. Turning compute into a fixed cost is also why gross margins look high.
Half emptyLittle of this shows in capex or on the balance sheet yet. Chips age fast and need replacing, and community pushback is making data centres dearer.
Risk→ inputs 04–05
Half full$114B of cash, plus about $100B from the IPO, covers commitments for the next few years. Clients who build Claude into their operations may find it hard to rip out.
Half emptyThe bonds are rated BB+. Fixed compute costs raise operating leverage, AI budgets may prove cyclical, and the company needs growth to meet its obligations.
Governance→ input 06
Half fullAs a public benefit corporation, it can put safety and its stated purpose ahead of short-term profit.
Half emptyFive share classes. Founders keep 50.1% of votes however many shares are issued, and a benefit trust picks the board. Insiders can sell 10% on day one; founders wait 365 days.

Under the hood

How the number is built

A 10-year discounted cash flow, built the way Damodaran structures his IPO valuations. Revenue grows from today's base to your 2036 target, with growth fading to the risk-free rate in years 6–10. Margins glide from break-even to your target by year 5. Each year's reinvestment is the extra revenue divided by your sales-to-capital ratio. A terminal value captures everything after 2036. The total is then haircut for the chance of failure, debt (including the present value of compute commitments) comes off, and cash (including IPO proceeds) goes on.

The governance discount is applied last, to the equity value, as the most you would pay for a share. It is not part of Damodaran's value; it is the extra discount he says Anthropic's structure requires.
