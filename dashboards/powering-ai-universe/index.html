AI Infrastructure

376 companies stand between AI ambition and physical reality.

Barclays mapped the pick-and-shovel universe behind a $1trn-a-year buildout across 19 subcategories. It is a list, not a view — every name sits on the same flat plane, whether it lives or dies on AI or merely brushes against it. This dashboard rebuilds that universe as a five-layer stack and scores each company on how much of it is genuinely levered to the buildout, how scarce what it sells actually is, and when the money arrives.

The stack, from the AI dollar outward

Barclays’ 19 subcategories are not a flat list — they are a causal chain. A dollar of hyperscaler capex enters at Layer 1 and works its way down to the molecule. The further down you go, the further from the AI narrative you sit, and the closer you get to the physical constraint that actually gates the buildout. Click any subcategory to filter the universe.

The asymmetry worth noticing: the layers with the most companies are not the layers with the tightest constraints. Attention pools at the top of the stack, where the story is cleanest; the binding shortages — turbine slots, large power transformers, and licensed electrical crews — sit two and three layers down, where the supplier count is thinnest and pricing power is most durable.

Purity against scarcity

The two questions that decide whether a name on this list is an AI investment or an AI anecdote. Horizontal: how much of the company is actually levered to the buildout. Vertical: how scarce what it sells is, relative to demand. Bubble area is market capitalisation; colour is stack layer. The filters above apply here too.
